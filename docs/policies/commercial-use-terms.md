# Commercial Use Terms

## 1. Purpose

These terms describe how the project may be used in commercial settings, including vendor deployments, service contracts, white-label builds, and paid support arrangements.

They are especially relevant because the repository is Apache 2.0, but the project identity and commercial packaging still need separate control.

## 2. Core Position

Commercial use of the source code may be allowed under Apache 2.0.

Commercial use of the **brand**, **project identity**, **official mission framing**, or **support relationship** may require separate written permission.

## 3. What Commercial Users May Need to Respect

Commercial deployers should respect:

- Apache 2.0 source-code obligations
- attribution and NOTICE requirements
- privacy and security expectations
- clinical safety and auditability rules
- trademark and branding restrictions
- any separate agreement covering support, hosting, customization, or white-label use

## 4. White-Label Deployments

A white-label deployment is not automatically permitted just because the code is open.

If a white-label deployment is approved, it should still:

- preserve required legal notices
- avoid false claims of upstream endorsement
- avoid confusing use of upstream names, marks, or domains
- maintain safety and compliance controls
- preserve a clear record of what was changed and by whom

## 5. Rebranding and Resale

A commercial partner may not:

- resell the product under the upstream identity in a misleading way
- imply exclusive relationship or ownership
- strip provenance to hide that the build is derivative
- use the project’s trust, public-good framing, or mission alignment as a marketing shortcut

## 6. Mission-Aligned Commercial Use

Commercial use is most aligned with the project when it supports:

- practitioners and care teams
- hospitals and institutions
- universities and training programmes
- NGO and public-benefit deployments
- underserved and low-resource communities
- interoperable mental-health infrastructure

## 7. India-Focused Operating Principles

For Indian deployments, commercial use should not undermine the public-interest goals of the mission-aligned ecosystem.

Practical expectations include:

- low-friction access for legitimate care teams
- support for collaborative workflows and referrals
- sensible pricing for public-benefit use cases
- no deceptive lock-in or identity confusion
- no false claim that a commercial build is an official national platform

## 8. Support and Services

Support, hosting, custom development, implementation, or training may be offered commercially if separately contracted.

Those services are separate from the Apache 2.0 source licence and do not create trademark rights or official endorsement.

## 9. Enforcement and Remedies

If a commercial deployment violates these terms or creates confusion, the project may:

- refuse support
- withdraw brand permission
- request corrective labelling
- request removal of misleading materials
- pursue legal remedies where available

## 10. Practical Rule

**Open code does not equal open brand.**

Commercial users may be able to reuse the code under Apache 2.0, but they still need permission for identity, endorsement, and public-facing brand use.
